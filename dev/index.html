OrdinalStatistics.OrdinalStatistics — Module
OrdinalStatistics

Location, variability, association, inequality and poverty measures for ordinal and categorical variables, following the work of R. Peñaloza and J. Souza.

All measures act on an OrdinalDistribution (relative frequencies over m categories ordered from worst to best) and also accept a plain vector of absolute or relative frequencies.

source
OrdinalStatistics.AmbiguousDualError — Type
AmbiguousDualError(d)

Thrown by dual and codual when the isotonia rule cannot assign the roots of the fundamental equation to the dual categories: the masses weakly below and above the centrum tie, and so do the numbers of categories weakly below and above it (e.g. f = (½, 0, 0, ½)). Pass ambiguous = :lower or ambiguous = :upper to choose explicitly.

source
OrdinalStatistics.OrdinalDistribution — Type
OrdinalDistribution(x::AbstractVector{<:Real})

An observation (X, ≺, m, f) of an ordinal variable: relative frequencies f ∈ Δ^{m-1} over m ≥ 2 categories x₁ ≺ x₂ ≺ … ≺ x_m, ordered from the worst (or lowest intensity) to the best (or highest intensity).

x may hold absolute or relative frequencies; it is normalized to sum to one. Entries must be finite, non-negative and not all zero. If x has an integer element type, it is read as absolute frequencies and the number of observations n = Σ xⱼ is stored (see nobs).

Every measure in this package accepts either an OrdinalDistribution or a plain frequency vector.

Examples

julia> d = OrdinalDistribution([7, 13, 13, 5, 8, 2, 0]);

julia> ncategories(d), nobs(d)
(7, 48)

Source: Souza & Peñaloza (2005), ch. II; Peñaloza (2012a).

OrdinalStatistics.centrum — Method
centrum(d; atol = √eps)

Centrum (ordinal expectation) of the observation: the category, or the pair of consecutive categories, nearest to m·S̄. Returned as a range of category positions: c:c for a single central category, c:c+1 when the fractional part of m·S̄ equals ½ (within atol).

\[\mathcal{E}_\prec(X) = \{x_c : c \in \varsigma(m\bar S)\},\qquad \varsigma(y) = \begin{cases} \lfloor y\rfloor, & y-\lfloor y\rfloor < \tfrac12,\\ \{\lfloor y\rfloor, \lfloor y\rfloor+1\}, & y-\lfloor y\rfloor = \tfrac12,\\ \lfloor y\rfloor+1, & y-\lfloor y\rfloor > \tfrac12. \end{cases}\]

Source: Souza & Peñaloza (2005), ch. II; Peñaloza (2012a).

Examples

julia> centrum([0.5, 0, 0, 0, 0.5])
3:3

julia> centrum([0.5, 0, 0, 0.5])
2:3

OrdinalStatistics.cindex — Method
cindex(qs; ambiguous = :error, atol = √eps)

Composite index (c-index) Γ(𝒬) of a questionnaire of ordinal questions: the average of the questions' ordinal_indexes weighted by their endogenous_weights,

\[\Gamma(\mathcal{Q}) = \sum_{q=1}^n w_q\,\mathcal{I}_q .\]

The keywords are forwarded to codual.

Source: Peñaloza (2012a); Peñaloza & Macedo (2012); Peñaloza (2013).

Examples

julia> qs = [[7, 13, 13, 5, 8, 2, 0], [11, 12, 17, 3, 1, 4, 0]];  # democracy, 1979

julia> round(cindex(qs), digits = 2)
30.55

OrdinalStatistics.codual — Method
codual(d; ambiguous = :error, atol = √eps)

Ordinal codual ⟨υ²⟩ᶜ = 1 - ⟨υ²⟩: the weight of the upper category of the dual series. Reducing the spectrum of answers to "low" and "high" ("off track" and "on track"), it is the degree of belief of being "on track". See dual for the definition and keywords.

Source: Peñaloza (2012a); Peñaloza & Macedo (2012).

Examples

julia> round(codual([0.25, 0, 0, 0, 0.75]), digits = 4)
0.8644

julia> round(codual([0.5, 0, 0, 0.5]; ambiguous = :upper), digits = 4)
0.7887

OrdinalStatistics.contribution — Method
contribution(qs; ambiguous = :error, atol = √eps)

Contribution Δ_q Γ = Γ − Γ_{−q} of each question to the cindex, where Γ_{−q} is the c-index without question q (weights recomputed):

\[\Delta_q\Gamma = \frac{w_q}{1 - w_q}\bigl(\mathcal{I}_q - \Gamma\bigr).\]

It is positive iff the question's index exceeds the c-index, and satisfies Σ_q (1 − w_q) Δ_q Γ = 0. Returns a vector with one entry per question; an entry is NaN when w_q = 1 (removing the question leaves no positive weight). The keywords are forwarded to codual.

Source: Peñaloza (2012a).

OrdinalStatistics.dual — Method
dual(d; ambiguous = :error, atol = √eps)

Ordinal dual ⟨υ²⟩ of the Gini–Simpson variability: the weight of the lower category of the dual series, a dichotomous series y₁ ≺ y₂ with weights (⟨υ²⟩, ⟨υ²⟩ᶜ) satisfying

  1. isovariability: the dual series has the same gini_simpson variability υ² as f, so ⟨υ²⟩ is a root of the fundamental equation of duality

    \[d^2 - d + \frac{\upsilon^2}{4} = 0, \qquad d_\pm = \frac{1 \pm \sqrt{1-\upsilon^2}}{2};\]

  2. isotonia: let x_c be the centrum (for a pair centrum {x_c, x_{c+1}}, use c below and c+1 above) and compare the mass weakly below it, Σ_{j≤c} fⱼ, with the mass weakly above it, Σ_{j≥c} fⱼ:
    • if the mass below is larger, ⟨υ²⟩ = d₊;
    • if the mass above is larger, ⟨υ²⟩ = d₋;
    • on a tie, compare the numbers of categories weakly below (c) and above (m − c + 1): more categories above (respondents "off track") gives ⟨υ²⟩ = d₊, more below gives ⟨υ²⟩ = d₋;
    • if these also tie, the dual is ambiguous.

ambiguous controls the ambiguous case: :error throws an AmbiguousDualError, :lower takes the smaller root as the codual (dual d₊), :upper the larger root as the codual (dual d₋). atol is the tolerance for the centrum and isotonia ties.

The codual is 1 - ⟨υ²⟩.

Source: Peñaloza (2012a); Peñaloza & Macedo (2012).

Examples

julia> round(dual([0.1, 0.5, 0.1, 0.2, 0.1]), digits = 4)
0.6936

OrdinalStatistics.endogenous_weights — Method
endogenous_weights(qs; ambiguous = :error, atol = √eps)

Endogenous weights of a questionnaire 𝒬 = {X₁, …, X_n} of ordinal questions: each question's share of the total codual,

\[w_q = \frac{\langle\upsilon^2_q\rangle^c}{\sum_{k=1}^n \langle\upsilon^2_k\rangle^c}.\]

qs is a collection of OrdinalDistributions or frequency vectors; questions may have different numbers of categories. The keywords are forwarded to codual. Throws a DomainError if every codual is zero.

Source: Peñaloza (2012a); Peñaloza & Macedo (2012).

OrdinalStatistics.gini_simpson — Method
gini_simpson(d)

Quadratic variability υ² ∈ [0, 1], the normalized Gini–Simpson index (GSI), also called the nominal variance:

\[\upsilon^2 = \frac{m}{m-1}\Bigl(1 - \sum_{j=1}^m f_j^2\Bigr).\]

It is 0 iff f is concentrated and 1 iff f is uniform; 1 - Σ fⱼ² is the proportion of ordered pairs of objects (with replacement) falling in different categories.

Source: Peñaloza (2011) (nominal variance); Peñaloza (2012a) (Gini–Simpson index).

Examples

julia> round(gini_simpson([0.02, 0.10, 0.70, 0.18]), digits = 4)
0.6229

OrdinalStatistics.index_thresholds — Method
index_thresholds(m::Integer)

Thresholds t₁ < … < t_{m-1} of the a-posteriori partition of [0, 100] induced by an ordinal variable with m categories:

\[t_h = \frac{100}{m-1}\Bigl(h - \frac12\Bigr),\qquad h = 1,\dots,m-1 .\]

The index lies in (t_{h-1}, t_h) iff the centrum is {x_h} (with t₀ = 0, t_m = 100, end cells closed at 0 and 100), and equals t_h iff the centrum is {x_h, x_{h+1}}. Inner cells have length 100/(m-1); the two end cells have half that length.

Source: Peñaloza (2012a); Peñaloza & Macedo (2012).

Examples

julia> index_thresholds(5)
4-element Vector{Float64}:
 12.5
 37.5
 62.5
 87.5

OrdinalStatistics.mean_charge — Method
mean_charge(d)

Mean charge S̄ (carga média; also mean post-cumulated frequency):

\[\bar S = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^m S_j = \sum_{j=1}^m \frac{j}{m} f_j, \qquad S_j = \sum_{k=j}^m f_k ,\]

where S_j is the post-cumulated frequency (distributional charge) of category xⱼ. It satisfies 1/m ≤ S̄ ≤ 1, and m·S̄ = k when f is concentrated in x_k.

Source: Souza (1977); Souza & Peñaloza (2005), ch. II; Peñaloza (2012a).

Examples

julia> mean_charge([0.1, 0.5, 0.1, 0.2, 0.1]) ≈ 0.54
true

OrdinalStatistics.modal_variability — Method
modal_variability(d)

Modal variability υ^∞ ∈ [0, 1]:

\[\upsilon^\infty = \frac{m}{m-1}\Bigl(1 - \max_{1\le j\le m} f_j\Bigr).\]

It is 0 iff f is concentrated and 1 iff f is uniform. It ignores changes outside the modal category; gini_simpson should usually be preferred.

Source: Souza & Peñaloza (2005), ch. II; Peñaloza (2011).

Examples

julia> modal_variability([0.02, 0.10, 0.70, 0.18]) ≈ 0.4
true

OrdinalStatistics.normalized_entropy — Method
normalized_entropy(d)

Normalized entropy ε ∈ [0, 1], with the convention 0 ln 0 = 0:

\[\varepsilon = \frac{E}{\ln m}, \qquad E = -\sum_{j=1}^m f_j \ln f_j .\]

It is 0 iff f is concentrated and 1 iff f is uniform.

Source: Souza & Peñaloza (2005), ch. II.

Examples

julia> round(normalized_entropy([0.02, 0.10, 0.70, 0.18]), digits = 4)
0.6253

OrdinalStatistics.ordinal_index — Method
ordinal_index(d)

The index ℐ(X) ∈ [0, 100] of an ordinal observation, the linear rescaling of the mean charge to [0, 100]:

\[\mathcal{I}(X) = 100\,\frac{\bar S - 1/m}{1 - 1/m} = 100\sum_{j=1}^m \frac{j-1}{m-1} f_j .\]

It equals the quality index ℋ of Ebserh (2015). The centrum is the category whose cell of the partition given by index_thresholds contains the index.

Source: Peñaloza (2012a); Peñaloza & Macedo (2012).

Examples

julia> ordinal_index([0.1, 0.5, 0.1, 0.2, 0.1]) ≈ 42.5
true

OrdinalStatistics.renyi_variability — Method
renyi_variability(d, θ::Real)

Rényi variability of order θ > 1, normalized to [0, 1]:

\[\upsilon^\theta = \frac{1 - \sum_{j=1}^m f_j^\theta}{1 - m^{1-\theta}} .\]

It is 0 iff f is concentrated and 1 iff f is uniform; θ = 2 gives gini_simpson.

Source: Souza & Peñaloza (2005), ch. II.

Examples

julia> d = OrdinalDistribution([0.1, 0.5, 0.1, 0.2, 0.1]);

julia> renyi_variability(d, 2) ≈ gini_simpson(d)
true
